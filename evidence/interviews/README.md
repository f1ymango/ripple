# Interviews

Owner: Impact. These notes answer one judging question directly — *"Has the
prototype been tested with potential users?"* — so they are worth more than
their length suggests.

## Who to talk to

People who route vehicles through Hong Kong roads daily, in rough order of how
easy they are to reach:

- driving instructors and taxi or minibus drivers on the Aberdeen corridor
- logistics or delivery dispatchers
- bus operator control-room staff
- a traffic or transport researcher

Twenty minutes each is enough. Two or three interviews is the target; one real
conversation beats three imagined ones.

## What to ask

Keep the questions about *today*, not about our idea. We want to hear what they
already do, in their words.

1. When a road you use closes, how do you find out?
2. What do you do in the first ten minutes?
3. Who tells you anything, and how much do you trust it?
4. When the advice says "use an alternative route", do you take it? Why not?
5. What would you want to know that nobody tells you today?
6. Who else is deciding at the same moment as you?

Only at the end, show the console or the brief and ask what they would do with
it. Note what they ignore as carefully as what they like.

## Ground rules

- Say who we are, that this is a student hackathon project, and what the notes
  will be used for. Ask before recording anything.
- No names, employers or contact details in this folder — write "a minibus
  driver, Aberdeen route" and keep identifying details out of the repo.
- Write what they said, not what we hoped they would say. A quote that
  contradicts our idea is more useful in the Q&A than one that flatters it.

## Files

One file per interview, named `YYYY-MM-DD-role.md`, e.g. `2026-09-28-minibus-driver.md`.
Each one: date, role, how long, what they do today, what they said (quotes where
possible), what surprised us, what it changes about the product.
